About the Watershed

Understanding the geographical identity of Bharathapuzha

Bharathapuzha, popularly known as Nila, is one of the most significant rivers of Kerala and the second-longest west-flowing river in the state. Originating in the Anamalai Hills of the Western Ghats, the river flows westward through parts of Tamil Nadu and Kerala before joining the Arabian Sea at Ponnani.

The Bharathapuzha watershed is an important hydrological unit that supports agriculture, settlements, biodiversity and various human activities. Its drainage system comprises several tributaries that contribute to the main river.

The watershed exhibits considerable geographical variation, extending from elevated mountainous terrain in the Western Ghats to the midlands, floodplains and coastal lowlands.

This WebGIS platform provides a spatial representation of the Bharathapuzha watershed and facilitates the exploration of its geographical and hydrological characteristics.

Geographical Information

Major geographical characteristics of the watershed

📍

Geographical Location

The basin extends across parts of Kerala and Tamil Nadu, covering Palakkad, Malappuram and Thrissur districts in Kerala and parts of Coimbatore region in Tamil Nadu.

🏔️

Origin and Relief

The river originates in the Anamalai Hills of the Western Ghats. The watershed includes highlands, midlands, lowlands and coastal landscapes.

🌊

Drainage System

The watershed contains a network of tributaries and streams that drain into the main river. It exhibits dendritic to sub-dendritic drainage characteristics.

🌧️

Climate and Rainfall

The basin experiences a tropical monsoon climate. The southwest monsoon contributes significantly to its annual rainfall and river discharge.

🌱

Land Use and Land Cover

Major land-cover categories include forests, plantations, agricultural lands, settlements, barren areas and water bodies.

🌾

Economic Significance

The river basin supports irrigation, agriculture, domestic water requirements, settlements and diverse livelihood activities.

Major Tributaries

The principal drainage contributors of Bharathapuzha

The Bharathapuzha river system is sustained by several tributaries originating from the Western Ghats and surrounding highlands. These tributaries contribute to the drainage network and influence the hydrological characteristics of the basin.

Importance of Watershed Mapping

GIS applications in watershed analysis and management

  • Drainage Analysis: Understanding the spatial distribution of streams, tributaries and drainage networks.
  • Watershed Management: Supporting sustainable management of surface water resources.
  • Flood Assessment: Identifying flood-prone regions and understanding spatial variations in flood risk.
  • Land Use Analysis: Examining the relationship between land-use patterns and watershed characteristics.
  • Environmental Conservation: Supporting the conservation of riverine ecosystems and natural resources.
  • Spatial Decision Making: Using GIS-based information to support planning and sustainable development.
